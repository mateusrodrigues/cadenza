# Cadenza privacy policy

_Effective [date of publishing]_

Cadenza is a browser extension that suggests changes to text you write on the web. It has no
server of its own. This policy explains what Cadenza reads, where it sends it, and what it
keeps.

## The short version

- Cadenza sends the text you are writing only to the AI provider you chose in its settings, or
  to a model on your own computer. It sends it nowhere else.
- The developer of Cadenza receives none of your data: no text, no keys, no usage and no
  analytics.
- Everything Cadenza keeps stays in your browser, and is removed when you uninstall it.

## What Cadenza reads

Cadenza reads the text of a field on a web page only while you are typing in it, and only
when Cadenza is switched on for that site. It never reads:

- password fields, payment card fields or one-time-code fields;
- code editors;
- Google Docs documents, which Cadenza doesn't support yet;
- kinds of field you turned off in its settings (text fields, text areas or rich editors);
- fields a website marks as not to be checked;
- anything on a site you have turned Cadenza off for, or anything at all while Cadenza is
  switched off.

## Where your text goes

When you pause typing, Cadenza sends the paragraphs you changed to the provider you set up:

- **A hosted AI provider.** The text goes from your browser straight to that provider, with
  your API key. The provider's own privacy policy and terms cover what it does with the text.
  Cadenza sends the text with instructions to suggest corrections. The provider's reply is
  used to show suggestions, and nothing else.
- **A model on your computer or your local network.** The text goes to that server and does
  not leave your machine or network.

Cadenza makes no other network requests. Chrome updates the extension from the Chrome Web
Store, as it does for all extensions.

## What Cadenza keeps, and where

All of this is stored in your browser's storage for the extension. Web pages cannot read it.

| What                                                         | How long                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Your settings, and the providers you added (without keys)    | Until you change them                      |
| Your API keys, encrypted if you set a passphrase             | Until you delete them                      |
| Recent suggestions, keyed by a fingerprint of each paragraph | In memory only; cleared when Chrome closes |
| Words you added to your dictionary                           | Until you remove them                      |
| Suggestions you asked not to see again, and the site         | Until you restore them                     |
| Sites you turned Cadenza off on, and the day you did         | Until you turn them back on                |
| How many checks you made each day, and the tokens they used  | 31 days                                    |

Whole paragraphs of your text are never stored. Recent suggestions hold only the words a
suggestion changes and its replacement, in memory, so the same text isn't checked twice.

Nothing is synced to your Google account or to other devices.

## Your choices

- Turn Cadenza off for all sites, or for one site, from its toolbar button or settings.
- Choose a provider, or a model on your own computer, at any time in settings.
- Delete a saved provider, and its key, in settings.
- Remove dictionary words, ignored suggestions and turned-off sites in settings.
- Uninstall Cadenza to remove everything it stored.

## What Cadenza doesn't do

Cadenza does not sell or share your data, use it for advertising, use it to decide credit or
lending, or use it for anything other than suggesting changes to your writing. It has no
analytics, crash reporting or tracking.
